\begin{abstract}
Violence detection in surveillance video has to run on many camera streams at
once, yet the most accurate models on the RWF-2000 benchmark use tens to
hundreds of millions of parameters. This paper studies how far a compact 3D
convolutional network can go within a budget of a few GFLOPs per clip.
MA-X3D extends X3D-M with two identity-initialised modules: a Motion
Attention gate driven by a multi-scale frame difference, and a
reparameterised $5\times5$ depthwise kernel in the first two residual stages
that is fused into a single kernel after training. The network is trained
with a two-phase discriminative schedule and distilled from a VideoMAE-B
teacher that is trained on the same clips. Because RWF-2000 has no
validation split and contains duplicated clips, we remove the duplicates,
group near-identical clips, and take every design decision by grouped
four-fold cross-validation, so that the test split is used once. Under this
protocol a single MA-X3D model with 3.0M parameters and 5.5 GFLOPs per view
reaches 87.6\% test accuracy and 0.875 F1 with one view, and 88.1\% with
four views, against 86.1\% for standard fine-tuning of the same network and
91.2\% for the 86M-parameter teacher.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The number of closed-circuit television cameras in public spaces has grown
faster than the capacity to watch them. By the end of 2019 an estimated 770
million surveillance cameras were in operation~\cite{cnbc2019}, and the
ability of an operator to notice rare events in a continuous monitoring task
falls within the first twenty minutes of a shift~\cite{mackworth1948}. An
automatic detector does not replace the operator; it selects the streams
that deserve attention.

Violent events are short, involve fast and irregular movement, may occupy a
small part of a wide-angle frame, and resemble non-violent activity such as
sport or horseplay. Work on the problem has followed action recognition in
general, from hand-crafted motion descriptors~\cite{idt,vif,hockey} to
two-stream networks with optical flow~\cite{twostream}, 3D convolutional
networks~\cite{i3d,slowfast,x3d}, and video
transformers~\cite{timesformer,videoswin}.

Cost is a further requirement. An installation carries tens or hundreds of
cameras, and a detector is useful only if it can run on all of them. The
highest accuracy reported on RWF-2000~\cite{rwf2000}, 94.0\%, is obtained by
CUE-Net~\cite{cuenet} with 354M parameters and about 5.8 TFLOPs per clip;
masked-autoencoder transformers report 91.8--92.4\% with 67M to 305M
parameters~\cite{videomae,mixvit}. A second line of work stays within a
small budget~\cite{rwf2000,sepconvlstm,mdcn,biltma}. This paper belongs to
the second line: the deployed model must cost a few GFLOPs per clip.

Three issues limit compact 3D backbones on this task. First, motion is only
implicit: the backbone infers movement from appearance changes, although the
frame difference is available at the cost of a subtraction and has been
shown to be an effective cue on this benchmark~\cite{sepconvlstm}. Existing
uses feed it to a second stream, which roughly doubles the cost. Second, the
early receptive field is narrow: the depthwise kernels of X3D are
$3\times3$ in space at every stage, including the high-resolution stages
where small and fast displacements are resolved. Third, with 1{,}600
training clips, fine-tuning a small backbone leaves a clear gap to large
pre-trained transformers, and reported numbers are hard to compare because
the benchmark has no validation split and the procedure used to select the
checkpoint is rarely stated.

We address the first two issues with MA-X3D, which attaches two small
modules to X3D-M~\cite{x3d}. Both start as an identity, so training begins
from the unmodified pre-trained backbone. Motion Attention turns a
multi-scale frame difference into four learnable temporal modes and uses
them to gate the Res4 features multiplicatively. The wide-kernel
convolution enlarges the depthwise kernels of Res2 and Res3 to $5\times5$
through a zero-initialised outer ring that is trained in its own
learning-rate group and fused into a single kernel after training. For the
third issue we train the network with a higher backbone learning rate and
with long, view-consistent distillation~\cite{beyer2022} from a VideoMAE-B
teacher, and we evaluate under a protocol in which the test split plays no
part in any decision.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a Motion Attention module that adds a frame-difference cue to a
        single 3D backbone with 2.5K parameters and no second stream;
  \item a wide-kernel reparameterisation that enlarges the depthwise
        kernels of the two earliest stages and leaves no training-time
        structure at inference; removing the learned ring at test time
        lowers accuracy from 87.6\% to 80.6\%, so the ring is used;
  \item a training recipe for a small video backbone on a small dataset,
        which raises single-model test accuracy from 86.1\% to 87.6\% (one
        view) without changing the network;
  \item a leakage-free evaluation of RWF-2000 (duplicate removal, grouped
        four-fold cross-validation, decisions on out-of-fold predictions,
        one use of the test split), with the split files and code released.
\end{itemize}

Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work, Section~\ref{sec:method}
describes the method, Section~\ref{sec:protocol} the protocol,
Section~\ref{sec:results} the results, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}
concludes.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Violence detection}

Hand-crafted methods encode motion along dense
trajectories~\cite{idt}, summarise changes of optical-flow
magnitude~\cite{vif}, or use acceleration descriptors~\cite{hockey}. They
are cheap but sensitive to viewpoint and lighting. Two-stream
networks~\cite{twostream} learn from RGB and optical flow; the flow is often
more expensive than the network. 3D convolutional
networks~\cite{c3d,i3d,slowfast,x3d} learn spatio-temporal features from
RGB alone, and video transformers~\cite{timesformer,videoswin} extend
self-attention to space and time at the price of large-scale pre-training.

On RWF-2000, ConvLSTM~\cite{convlstm} reports 77.00\% and
C3D~\cite{c3d} 82.75\%. The Flow Gated Network~\cite{rwf2000} gates an RGB
branch with an optical-flow branch and reaches 87.25\%.
SepConvLSTM~\cite{sepconvlstm} processes background-suppressed frames and
frame differences with two MobileNetV2 backbones and reaches 89.75\%. A
two-stream multi-dimensional network~\cite{mdcn} reaches 89.70\% below one
million parameters, and an EfficientNet-B0 with bidirectional motion
attention and temporal shift~\cite{biltma} reaches 90.25\% at 4.48M
parameters. VideoMAE transformers reach 91.8--92.4\%~\cite{videomae,mixvit}
and CUE-Net~\cite{cuenet} 94.00\%. Most of these works report accuracy on
the official test split and do not state how the checkpoint was chosen; we
return to this point in Section~\ref{sec:protocol}.

\subsection{Efficient video backbones}

X3D~\cite{x3d} expands a small 2D network one axis at a time (duration,
frame rate, resolution, depth, width, bottleneck width) and keeps the
expansion with the best accuracy-to-cost ratio. X3D-M reaches 76.0\% top-1
on Kinetics-400 with 3.8M parameters and 6.2 GFLOPs per view, comparable to
SlowFast R50 with nine times fewer parameters. X3D-S and X3D-M share the
same parameter count and differ in input resolution, which makes X3D-M the
most accurate member of the family within the smallest parameter budget.

\subsection{Motion cues, gating and reparameterisation}

Under a static camera the absolute difference between frames isolates the
movement of people, at a fraction of the cost of optical flow.
Multiplicative gating~\cite{se,cbam} re-weights features with a learned
signal; when the last layer of the gate starts near zero, the module starts
as an identity and does not disturb pre-trained features. Structural
reparameterisation~\cite{repvgg,acnet} trains extra branches and fuses them
into one kernel for inference. Both cited methods fuse back into the
original kernel shape; our ring fuses into a larger kernel, so the added
spatial support remains.

\subsection{Knowledge distillation}

Distillation trains a small student to match the softened outputs of a
larger teacher~\cite{hinton2015}. Beyer~et~al.~\cite{beyer2022} show that
the student benefits when teacher and student see the same augmented view
and when the schedule is much longer than for standard training. We follow
both observations with a video transformer as teacher and a 3D CNN as
student, and train one teacher per cross-validation fold so that no
validation clip is seen by either model.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}

The task is clip-level binary classification. A clip is an RGB tensor
$x \in [0,1]^{3 \times T \times H \times W}$ with $T=16$ and $H=W=224$. The
motion map is computed inside the network from $x$, so the model takes RGB
only. Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the pipeline. Four principles guide the
design. \textbf{P1 Lightweight}: no second backbone; the two modules add
36.2K parameters at inference. \textbf{P2 Identity initialisation}: at
epoch zero the model computes the same function as the pre-trained
backbone. \textbf{P3 No inference overhead}: training-time structure is
fused away. \textbf{P4 Training-only teacher}: the teacher is not part of
the deployed model.

\subsection{Input pipeline}

\textbf{Person-centred crop.} Offline, YOLOv8n~\cite{yolov8} detects people
on 12 of 64 frames spread over the clip, the box centres are clustered with
DBSCAN~\cite{dbscan}, and the box of the largest cluster, enlarged by 20\%,
is applied to every frame. The crop is static, so under a fixed camera the
background stays registered and the frame difference reflects the movement
of people rather than of the crop window. Frames are denoised with a
bilateral filter~\cite{bilateral}, contrast-enhanced with
CLAHE~\cite{clahe}, and resized to $224\times224$. The stored clip has 64
frames.

\textbf{Temporal sampling.} At test time the 16 frames are spread evenly
over the stored clip. During training they are spread over a random window
that covers 60--100\% of the clip, with $\pm1$ frame of jitter, so that
training and testing see the same time scale. An earlier sampler that drew
16 frames from 11--24 consecutive stored frames made the validation loss
rise after a few epochs.

\textbf{Augmentation.} Each random parameter is drawn once per clip and
applied to all frames, which keeps the frames registered: brightness
($\times0.5$--$1.5$), saturation ($\pm30\%$), a value shift, horizontal
flip, random crop (70--100\% of the side, $p{=}0.8$), rotation ($\pm10^\circ$,
$p{=}0.8$), a speed change ($\times0.7$--$1.3$, $p{=}0.5$) and Gaussian blur
($p{=}0.2$). CutMix~\cite{cutmix} is applied to half of the batches.

\subsection{Motion Attention}
\label{sec:ma}

Let $d^{(s)}_t = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{c=1}^{3} |x_{c,t} - x_{c,t-s}|$ be the
channel-averaged absolute frame difference at step $s$. The motion map is
\begin{equation}
  m_t = \min\!\left( \max\!\left(d^{(1)}_t,\, d^{(2)}_t\right),\; 0.2 \right) \times 5 \,,
  \label{eq:motion}
\end{equation}
computed on the $[0,1]$ frames before Kinetics normalisation. The two steps
capture fast and slower movement; the threshold 0.2 saturates flicker and
compression artefacts and maps typical differences to about $[0,1]$.

A temporal convolution with kernel $(3,1,1)$ maps $m$ to $N=4$ channels,
$M = \mathrm{TConv}(m)$. It mixes information only along time, so each
channel can represent a different temporal pattern. The first channel is
initialised as an impulse on the current frame, which reproduces $m$; the
others are drawn from $\mathcal{N}(0, 0.05^2)$. The four modes are resized
to the Res4 resolution by trilinear interpolation, giving $\tilde{M} \in
\mathbb{R}^{4 \times T' \times 14 \times 14}$, and a gate $g$ ($1{\times}1{\times}1$
convolution to 24 channels, ReLU, $1{\times}1{\times}1$ convolution to 96
channels, $\tanh$) modulates the Res4 output $f$:
\begin{equation}
  y = f \odot \bigl(1 + g(\tilde{M})\bigr) \,.
  \label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
The last convolution of $g$ starts from $\mathcal{N}(0, 0.01^2)$ with zero
bias, so $y \approx f$ at the start (P2). The multiplier lies in $[0,2]$:
the motion cue re-weights features the backbone has computed and cannot
replace them. Res4 is chosen because at $14\times14$ its features are
semantic and still resolve position; Res5 at $7\times7$ is too coarse to
localise movement. The module has 2{,}508 parameters.

\subsection{Wide-kernel reparameterised convolution}
\label{sec:wk}

The depthwise convolutions of Res2 and Res3 have $3\times3\times3$ kernels
and run at $56\times56$ and $28\times28$. We widen their spatial support to
$5\times5$ as
\begin{equation}
  W_{\text{eff}} = \mathrm{Pad}_{3 \to 5}(W_{\text{base}})
    + M_{\text{ring}} \odot W_\delta \,,
  \label{eq:rep}
\end{equation}
where $W_{\text{base}}$ is the pre-trained kernel padded to $5\times5$,
$W_\delta$ is initialised to zero, and $M_{\text{ring}}$ is a fixed mask
that is one on the 16 outer positions of the $5\times5$ grid. The centre of
$W_{\text{eff}}$ therefore always equals the pre-trained kernel plus its own
fine-tuning, and the layer starts as the original convolution (P2).

Because $W_{\text{base}}$ and $W_\delta$ are separate tensors, the ring is
placed in the learning-rate group of the new modules ($5\times10^{-4}$),
ten times the backbone rate. With a single tensor, the ring would receive
the backbone rate; under AdamW the step size follows the learning rate
rather than the gradient scale, so the ring would move little.
After training the two terms are summed into one $5\times5$ kernel (P3);
the fused and unfused networks differ by less than $4\times10^{-7}$ in
output probability. The widening covers all eight depthwise convolutions of
Res2 (three blocks) and Res3 (five blocks) and adds 33{,}696 parameters
after fusion. Per stage of $L$ blocks, the spatial receptive field grows
from $2L$ to $4L$ pixels.

\subsection{Training}

\textbf{Two-phase fine-tuning.} In the probe phase (4 epochs) the backbone
is frozen and only the new linear classifier and Motion Attention are
trained at $10^{-3}$, with one warm-up epoch. In the fine-tuning phase
Res2, Res3, Res5 and the head are trained; the stem and Res4 stay frozen,
and so do the BatchNorm statistics of frozen layers. Pre-trained weights use
$5\times10^{-5}$ and the new parameters (classifier, Motion Attention,
rings) $5\times10^{-4}$. We use AdamW~\cite{adamw} (weight decay
$5\times10^{-4}$), a cosine schedule, label smoothing~\cite{labelsmoothing}
of 0.1, gradient clipping at 1.0, bf16 arithmetic, batch size 12, and an
exponential moving average of the weights (decay 0.999) that is used for
validation and for the final model. The optimiser is rebuilt at the phase
change. Training runs for at most 48 epochs and stops after 16 epochs
without an improvement of the validation F1.

\textbf{Distillation.} Let $s$ and $t$ be the student and teacher logits.
The loss is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = (1-\alpha)\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(y, s)
  + \alpha\, \tau^2\, \mathrm{KL}\!\left(\sigma(t/\tau) \,\|\, \sigma(s/\tau)\right),
  \label{eq:kd}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.5$, $\tau = 2$ and $\sigma$ the softmax; with CutMix,
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$ is mixed with the CutMix weights. The teacher is
VideoMAE-B~\cite{videomae} pre-trained on Kinetics-400, fine-tuned for at
most 15 epochs on the training clips of the same fold (backbone
$3\times10^{-5}$, head $10^{-3}$, weight decay 0.05). It runs in inference
mode on the same augmented, CutMix-ed clip as the student, as recommended
in~\cite{beyer2022}. The code refuses a teacher whose training clips differ
from the student's.

\subsection{Inference}

The ring is fused and one model is evaluated. A clip is classified from one
view (the 16 evenly spread frames) or from the average of four views whose
frame positions are shifted by a quarter of the sampling stride. The
decision threshold is 0.5 (Section~\ref{sec:options}).

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\textbf{Data audit.} RWF-2000~\cite{rwf2000} provides 1{,}600 training and
400 test clips of about five seconds, half of them violent, collected from
YouTube. We compared all clips with frame descriptors. Nine training clips
are exact copies of test clips (all non-violent), and nine pairs of exact
copies occur within the training split. We remove 17 training clips so that
no test clip and no second copy is used for training, which leaves 1{,}583.
At a descriptor cosine similarity of at least 0.9, 31 training pairs show
the same scene; six of them carry different labels. These pairs are
grouped, giving 1{,}572 groups of at most three clips.

\textbf{Cross-validation.} The benchmark has no validation split. We split
the 1{,}583 clips into four folds that are stratified by class and never
separate a group (about 1{,}187 training and 396 validation clips per
fold). In each fold the checkpoint with the best validation F1 is kept.
The four validation sets cover every clip once, which gives one
out-of-fold (OOF) prediction per clip.

\textbf{Decisions and test use.} Every choice in this paper, including the
training recipe, the number of views and the threshold, is made on the
1{,}583 OOF predictions. The test split is then scored once. Main results
report the test accuracy of a single model as the mean $\pm$ standard
deviation over the four fold models; each of them has seen 75\% of the
training clips. We also report the average of the four models where
stated, with its cost.
% TODO: 3 seeds x 4 folds once the runs finish; state "mean +- std over 12 models".

\textbf{Metrics and cost.} We report accuracy, and precision, recall and F1
of the violent class, and ROC-AUC. Cost is measured as parameters after
fusion, GFLOPs per view at $16\times224\times224$ (fvcore), latency and
throughput on one AMD MI250 GCD, CPU latency on an AMD EPYC 7413 with 16
threads, and training time and peak memory.

\textbf{Implementation.} PyTorch 2.14 on ROCm 7.2. The depthwise 3D
convolutions use custom Triton kernels, which make a training step 2.3
times faster than the vendor library and give the same results. One fold
trains on one MI250 GCD.

\subsection{Inference options}
\label{sec:options}

On the OOF predictions, four views raise accuracy by 0.25 points over one
view (0.4 points on the test split) at four times the cost. Horizontal-flip
test-time augmentation lowers OOF accuracy slightly, and tuning the
threshold on $[0.30, 0.70]$ selects 0.50--0.52 with no measurable gain. We
therefore use one view and a threshold of 0.5 for deployment and report
four views as an option.

\subsection{Cost}

Table~\ref{tab:cost} compares the deployed student with the teacher. The
student has 29 times fewer parameters and 25 times fewer FLOPs, and is
about twice as fast on CPU. On the MI250 GPU the two models have similar
latency and throughput: depthwise 3D convolutions are limited by memory
bandwidth on this hardware, while the dense matrix products of the
transformer run close to peak. The advantage of the student is therefore
largest on CPUs and memory-limited devices.

%======================================================================
\section{Limitations}

The results come from one benchmark whose test split has 400 clips, so one
point of accuracy is four clips. For every model, including the teacher,
test accuracy is 1--3 points below OOF accuracy, which suggests that the
test clips are harder than the training clips. The static crop assumes a
fixed camera. Training requires a teacher that is 29 times larger than the
student. The model classifies trimmed clips and does not localise events in
time. The Hockey Fight results of our earlier work and the effect of the
person-centred crop were not re-evaluated under the present protocol.

%======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

MA-X3D adds a frame-difference gate and a fused wide kernel to X3D-M at a
cost of 36K parameters. Trained with a higher backbone learning rate and
long, view-consistent distillation from a video transformer, a single model
with 3.0M parameters reaches 87.6\% on RWF-2000 with one view, under a
protocol in which the test split takes no part in any decision. Future work
includes temporal localisation on untrimmed streams, measurements on edge
devices, and cross-dataset evaluation under the same protocol.
